|---|---|

## Discrepancies to know about
- Head count: Grace's Guide quotes a period report of "about 70 officers and men"; a Taylor & Francis text says 60. Script uses 74 (Wikipedia / Manchester Guardian).
- Date: the Grace's Guide newspaper extract says "Monday the 11th"; most sources say 12 April. Script says only "1831", so this doesn't matter.
- The script says "16 feet" (lower of the 16-18 ft figure).
- "73 other soldiers" = the 74 of the detachment minus "you".
- "Before anyone can stop, an iron column falls": sources give the sound, then the column falling, with no gap
  described; the line claims no more than that.
- "Every step, that bolt pulls a little harder": framing for the mechanism (rhythmic load on the badly forged bolt),
  not a recorded observation. The bolt is real (investigation); the script never says anyone knew about it.
- "and nobody dies": true (no deaths; 20 injured, not spoken in this draft).
- "Every step, the bridge swings higher": the sources say the men stepped to humour the vibration before the
  collapse; growing motion is the mechanism the swing analogy explains, not a quoted observation.
- "A bridge in London still has that sign": Albert Bridge (built 1873) carries a break-step notice; the script
  does not claim the sign dates from 1831.
- Uniform colour (dark rifle green) is a scene note, not narration: check a period source before the cast is built.

## Comic exaggeration (framing only, not presented as fact)
- "The British Army can march anywhere, except across a bridge" (true rule: troops break step, i.e. stop
  marching in step, when crossing; the script states the real rule two lines before the end)
- "and it's all your fault" (you are one of the 74; the bolt was also to blame, see the bolt line)
- The cold open's modern column shuffling across a bridge is a visual gag, not a claim about current drill
- "all of you think it's hilarious" (sources: they found it amusing)
- "the best-timed push a bridge has ever felt"
- "You just made eventually happen today."
- "telling soldiers not to do what you did" (the real sign says to break step when crossing)
