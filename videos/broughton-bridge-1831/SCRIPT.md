## Facts

- 12 April 1831, Broughton Suspension Bridge over the River Irwell, Salford near Manchester; opened 1826.
- Suspension bridges were then called the 'new wonder of the age'; the bridge was a source of local pride.
- A detachment of 74 men of the 60th Rifle Corps, returning to barracks from exercises on Kersal Moor, marching four abreast.
- They felt it vibrate in time with their steps, found it amusing, whistled a marching tune and stepped to 'humour it'.
- Near the far side they heard a sound like an irregular discharge of firearms; an iron column fell and the corner dropped 16-18 ft into the river.
- About 40 men thrown into water about 2 ft deep; no deaths; 20 injured.
- Investigation: a single badly forged bolt in a stay chain snapped; the bridge would likely have failed eventually anyway.
- Result: the British Army ordered troops to break step when crossing bridges; 'break step' signs still exist (e.g. Albert Bridge, London).
